# Portfolio Selection Using Empirical Distributions

## Overview

This project evaluates stock performance using empirical data to determine an optimal investment strategy based on expected return and risk.

## Scenario

An investor is analyzing three stocks:

* Home Depot (HD)
* Walmart (WMT)
* Amazon (AMZN)

The goal is to construct a portfolio based on historical performance over a three-month period (April–July).

## Methodology

For each stock:

* Monthly percentage changes were calculated
* Mean return was used as a measure of expected return
* Variability in returns was used as an indicator of risk

## Data Summary

### Home Depot (HD)

* Mean return: 1.44%
* Observed both positive and negative monthly changes

### Walmart (WMT)

* Mean return: 1.10%
* Relatively stable but low growth

### Amazon (AMZN)

* Mean return: 9.71%
* Consistently strong positive performance

## Results

Amazon significantly outperformed the other two stocks in terms of average return, while also maintaining consistent growth.

## Investment Decision

Based on the analysis:

* A higher allocation should be made to Amazon
* Lower allocations should be given to Home Depot and Walmart

## Limitations

* Short time horizon (3 months)
* Simplified risk measurement
* Does not include diversification strategies

## Conclusion

This project demonstrates how empirical data can guide portfolio decisions. While Amazon appears to be the strongest performer, a more comprehensive strategy would incorporate longer time horizons and diversification considerations.
